# Real-time aircraft data for SFO Live 3D: sources, terms and measurements

Research note, 24 Sep 2026 (UTC). Scope: which source(s) of real-time aircraft state should drive the live 3D view of
KSFO (approaches, runway used, go-arounds, take-offs, taxiing, parking), and how to merge them.

- **Observed** means we measured it ourselves, from the recordings or a probe made on 24 Sep 2026 from this sandbox.
  The sandbox's egress IP is a shared cloud address (Cloudflare edge `IAD`).
- **Documented** means it is quoted from the provider's own page, with the URL and retrieval date.
- **Inferred** means it is our interpretation.
- **Unverified** means we could not reach the original source.

Every number in section 4 is regenerated by `python3 tools/live/analyze_feeds.py --markdown`. That tool reads the
recordings in `refs/cache/rec/` and writes `refs/cache/feeds/analysis.json`. All third-party pages and data cited
here are saved under `refs/cache/feeds/`, which is gitignored.

---

## 1. Recommendation

1. **Primary source: the adsb.lol API** — `GET https://api.adsb.lol/v2/point/{lat}/{lon}/{radius_nm}`, reached through the
   local relay (`sfo_live_server.py`, HANDOFF P2).
   - Why: it is the freshest free source. Its snapshot `now` advances in 0.5 s steps and is generated at the moment of
     the request. Positions of approaching aircraft are about 0.15 s old at request time (median).
   - Licence: the data is ODbL 1.0, which has clear public-use rules (attribution; share-alike only for derived
     databases).
   - Catches:
     - About 20 % of requests at 1 request/s got HTTP 429 (observed). The documented rate limits are "dynamic".
     - The docs ask production users to make contact, and say an API key tied to feeding will be required "in the
       future".
2. **Secondary source, merged continuously (not only a fallback): the adsb.fi open data API** —
   `GET https://opendata.adsb.fi/api/v3/lat/{lat}/lon/{lon}/dist/{nm}`.
   - Polling: every **2.0 s**. Its snapshot only changes on even seconds, so polling faster only returns duplicates
     (observed: 50 % duplicates at 1 request/s).
   - What it adds:
     - only 6 isolated 429s in 3,961 requests at 1 request/s;
     - a few parked aircraft at the gates that adsb.lol did not have (section 4.4);
     - richer aircraft-database fields (`desc`, `ownOp`, `year`).
   - Licence: personal, non-commercial use, with a citation and link.
   - Use the **v3** endpoint. The recorder currently uses v2, which is deprecated.
3. **Merge policy: freshest position per ICAO hex wins** (section 5.2):
   - Position time = `now − seen_pos`, computed per provider.
   - Every other field comes from the freshest snapshot that carries it.
   - Keep per-aircraft provenance, because attribution and licence obligations differ by provider.
   - Merged, the median approach position is 0.25 s old relative to the newest snapshot. The merged update interval is
     about 1.1 s airborne and while taxiing (section 4.3).
4. **Altitude.** The scene is geometric, so place aircraft by **geometric height**.
   - Use `MSL_ft = alt_geom + 105.9` when `alt_geom` is present. It was present in 100 % of approach reports.
     - `alt_geom` is WGS84-ellipsoidal (HAE), as readsb documents. We confirmed this below 1000 ft: it sits a median
       72 ft below the QNH altitude, against 106 ft expected from EGM96 (section 4.6).
     - The QNH-corrected barometric altitude drifts from geometric height with temperature. Tonight it ran up to
       ~130 ft low at 4000–5000 ft.
   - Fall back to `alt_baro` + QNH, plus that aircraft's last known geometric−baro offset.
     - QNH source: the KSFO METAR altimeter. The aircraft's own `nav_qnh` agreed with it within 0.1 hPa.
     - The rule: 14 CFR 91.121 below 18,000 ft MSL.
   - On the ground `alt_baro` is the string `"ground"`. Pin the aircraft to the airport surface model.
5. **Optional third input: a user-owned receiver** (RTL-SDR + readsb, plus dump978 for UAT).
   - It is the only way to get sub-second latency with no rate limits and no third-party terms.
   - Feeding adsb.lol and adsb.fi also unlocks their feeder-only streams: adsb.lol re-api and BEAST/SBS output, and
     the adsb.fi `/v2/snapshot` endpoint.
   - It merges with the same freshest-wins rule.
6. **Not suitable as live position sources:**
   - OpenSky: its licence forbids operational or live-product use without a written licence. Anonymous time
     resolution is 10 s, and it saw only 5–6 of the 18–22 SFO ground aircraft.
   - FlightAware AeroAPI and Firehose: per-query pricing, 10 result sets/min on the Personal tier; Firehose is
     enterprise only.
   - Flightradar24 API: 6–8 credits per aircraft per call, 10 calls/min and 20 results per call on the $9 plan. Its
     ToS also forbids using it "to supplement or backfill any other … real-time flight data".
   - ADS-B Exchange: the acceptable-use policy forbids redistribution; it is paid.
   - AeroDataBox: a schedule/status API, not a position feed.
   - airplanes.live: HTTP 403 "contact us".
   - **Worth using for enrichment or ground truth:**
     - AeroAPI (gate, terminal, `actual_runway_on`) or AeroDataBox (gate, terminal) for individual flights. This helps
       validate stand matching and runway detection; see sections 3.6 and 3.8.
     - FAA SWIM SCDS / STDDS for **ASSC surface tracks at SFO**. It covers aircraft *and vehicles*. It is free but
       needs an FAA account, an agreement and LADD compliance. Not tested.

**Attribution text for the UI** (it meets ODbL §4.3 and the adsb.fi terms; it must be visible wherever live data is
shown):

> Aircraft data: [ADSB.lol](https://adsb.lol) — contains information from ADSB.lol, which is made available here under
> the [Open Database License (ODbL)](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/) — and [adsb.fi](https://adsb.fi)
> open data (personal, non-commercial use). Routes: ADSB.lol / [VRS standing data](https://github.com/vradarserver/standing-data) (CC0).

## 2. What the app needs from a feed

| Need | Fields (readsb `jv2` names) | Minimum rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Approach, landing runway, go-around | `lat`, `lon`, `alt_baro`, `baro_rate`, `gs`, `track`, `nav_qnh`, `alt_geom` | ~1 Hz | The runways are 228 m apart. Assigning the runway needs cross-track error well under 100 m; measured about 5 m (section 4.7). |
| Take-off | the same, plus the air/ground state (`alt_baro == "ground"`) | ~1 Hz | Ground→air transition: section 4.7. |
| Taxiing | `lat`, `lon`, `gs`, `track`, `true_heading` | ~1 Hz | Surface position messages come at 2 Hz while moving and 0.2 Hz when still; see the note below this table. |
| Parking | `lat`, `lon`, `true_heading`, `gs == 0`, persistence after the transponder is switched off | 0.2 Hz is enough | Heading is often missing; the jitter is up to ~20 m (section 4.5). |
| Identity | `hex`, `flight`, `r`, `t`, `desc`, `ownOp`, `squawk`, `category` | once | `r`, `t`, `desc` and `ownOp` come from the aggregator's aircraft database, not from the aircraft. |
| Altimetry | `alt_geom` (HAE), or `alt_baro` with `nav_qnh` / the KSFO METAR altimeter | per report / per minute | Geoid at the ARP: EGM96 N = −32.29 m (section 4.6). |

**Transmission rates.** "Surface position 0.2 Hz (still) / 2 Hz (moving); airborne position 2 Hz" (ADS-B version 2).
Source: Table 1.4 of *The 1090 MHz Riddle*, 2nd ed., <https://mode-s.org/1090mhz/content/ads-b/1-basics.html> (open
access; retrieved 2026-09-24). The primary source, RTCA DO-260B, is paywalled and was not checked.

**Heading resolution.** Surface heading/track is 7-bit, "rounded to 360/128 degrees"
(<https://mode-s.org/1090mhz/content/ads-b/4-surface-position.html>). readsb decodes it as `true_heading` or `track`
depending on the aircraft's operational-status bits (readsb `mode_s.c` `decodeESSurfacePosition`, commit 843d8e3).

**Field meanings.** These are from readsb `README-json.md`, commit 843d8e3, <https://github.com/wiedehopf/readsb/blob/843d8e3/README-json.md>:
- "seen_pos: how long ago (in seconds before "now") the position was last updated"
- "alt_geom: geometric (GNSS / INS) altitude in feet referenced to the WGS84 ellipsoid"
- "true_heading: … (usually only transmitted on ground …)"
- "lastPosition: … when the regular lat and lon are older than 60 seconds"
- "now: the time the api data was cached … api caching frequency is controlled by --write-json-every". This is why the
  two providers' `now` values advance in different steps.

**Why MLAT does not matter at SFO.** Since 1 Jan 2020, ADS-B Out is required in Class B airspace (14 CFR 91.225(d)(1),
<https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.225>). Consistent with that, 100 % of approach reports were `adsb_icao` and
none of the SFO ground reports were MLAT (observed).

## 3. Candidates: access, limits, cost and terms

Retrieved 24 Sep 2026 unless stated. Quotes are verbatim.

### 3.1 adsb.lol (primary)

**Endpoints** (live OpenAPI, <https://api.adsb.lol/api/openapi.json>):
- `/v2/point/{lat}/{lon}/{radius}` (radius ≤ 250 NM) and `/v2/lat/{lat}/lon/{lon}/dist/{radius}`;
- `/v2/hex|callsign|reg|type|sqk|mil|ladd|pia`;
- `POST /api/0/routeset`.
- [corrected by verifier] The live OpenAPI (version 0.0.2, re-fetched 09:13 UTC) also lists `/v2/closest/{lat}/{lon}/{radius}`,
  `/v2/icao/{icao_hex}`, `/v2/registration/{registration}`, `/v2/squawk/{squawk}`, `/api/0/airport/{icao}`, `/0/me` and
  `/0/my`. `radius` is typed **integer**, 0–250.

The source is <https://github.com/adsblol/api>, commit 3c969c8. The API proxies the readsb re-api with the `jv2` flag.

**Terms of Service** (live OpenAPI description):
> "You can use the API for free. In the future, you will require an API key which you can get by feeding to adsb.lol.
> If you want to use the API for production purposes, please contact me so I do not break your application by accident."

**Licence** (same page):
> "The license for the API as well as all data ADSB.lol makes public is ODbL"

It links <https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/summary/> (the ODbL 1.0 summary page), not the 1.0 legal text
[corrected by verifier]. The feeder-only pages state "License: [ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1.0/)"
explicitly (adsblol/website ad6e769, `content/en/docs/feeders-only/re-api.md` and `beast-mlat-out.md`).

**Rate limits.** The repository README (commit 3c969c8) says:
> "Rate limits are dynamic based on the environment load. If you get 4xx errors, you are doing something wrong."

The deployed description no longer contains this sentence.

**Disclaimer** (<https://github.com/adsblol/website/blob/ad6e769/content/en/privacy-license/index.md>):
> "the data is provided on an “as is” basis"

**Feeder-only streams** (<https://www.adsb.lol/docs/feeders-only/re-api/>, <https://www.adsb.lol/docs/feeders-only/beast-mlat-out/>):
- "re-api is the readsb HTTP API, available to all ADSB.lol feeders … unfiltered and unmodified … only accessible by
  your station’s IP address"
- "Our aggregated data is available to all feeders. Port: 1365 (BEAST) and 1366 (MLAT, SBS) … This feature is
  experimental."

**Routes.** `POST /api/0/routeset` with `{"planes":[{"callsign","lat","lng"}]}` returns the route plus a `plausible`
flag. Its source is the VRS standing data, which is **CC0 1.0** (<https://github.com/vradarserver/standing-data/blob/main/LICENSE>).
These routes are schedule-based guesses, not flight plans (inferred from the code: `api_routes.py`).

**ODbL §4.3** (<https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/>):
> "if you Publicly Use a Produced Work, You must include a notice … reasonably calculated to make any Person that uses,
> views … the Produced Work aware that Content was obtained from the Database … and that it is available under this
> License."

The licence's example notice:
> "Contains information from DATABASE NAME, which is made available here under the Open Database License (ODbL)."

§4.4 is share-alike: any *Derivative Database* that is publicly used must be ODbL too. For example, a recorded
snapshot shipped in the artifact.

**Observed:**
- `now` advances in 0.5 s steps.
- 19.8 % of requests got HTTP 429 at 1 request/s. The rate was 21 % even when the previous request was ≥ 3 s
  earlier. So this is not a simple 1 request/s bucket: it is load-dependent, or other users share this egress
  IP (inferred).
- The response carries **no `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`**, even when an `Origin` header is sent. A browser cannot
  call it directly, so the relay is required.
- One request's round trip from here was 0.43 s.
- The response lacks `desc`, `ownOp` and `year`.

### 3.2 adsb.fi (secondary)

**Source:** the README at <https://github.com/adsbfi/opendata>, commit 9fe1c9a (2026-05-17). The adsb.fi web pages are
behind a Cloudflare challenge and could not be fetched.

**Endpoints:**
- `/v3/lat/[lat]/lon/[lon]/dist/[dist]` — "Returns aircraft within specified distance up to 250 NM".
- On v2: "The v2/lat/lon/dist endpoint returned data in a different format than other v2 endpoints. It remains
  functional for backward compatibility but is considered deprecated. The corresponding v3 endpoint should be used for
  all new integrations."
- v3 returns `ac`, with `now` in ms. The deprecated v2, which the recorder uses, returns `aircraft` with `now` in s.
- Feeder endpoint: `/v2/snapshot`, "all aircraft refreshed twice a minute".

**Limits:**
> "The public endpoints are rate limited to 1 request per second, and the feeder endpoint to 1 request every 30 seconds.
> Making excessive invalid HTTP requests results in a temporary IP address restriction. Requests returning a 400, 401,
> 403, 404, or 429 status code count toward the limit."

**Terms:**
> "adsb.fi open data is for personal, non-commercial use only. You may not license, sell, rent, or lease any part of
> the data or the service. The data and the service are provided as-is, without any warranty. You must cite adsb.fi and
> include a link to our home page. We reserve the right to suspend or terminate the service or access to it."

Also: "Please contact us if you have commercial or higher request rate requirements."

**Observed:**
- 6 isolated 429s in 3,961 requests (0.15 %) at 1 request/s.
  - Each came exactly 1.000 s after the previous request.
  - None coincided with our own probes (07:41 and 07:47 UTC).
  - So the 1 request/s limit is enforced tightly; polling every 2 s avoids it (inferred).
- `now` is always a whole even second (v2 and v3 alike). Three v3 probes returned `now` = …668000, …672000 and
  …676000 ms. So the data refreshes every **2 s**.
- The response carries no `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`, even with an `Origin` header.
- Round trip was 0.26–0.46 s over three probes.

**Inferred:** the adsb.fi terms forbid *licensing* the data. So adsb.fi-derived records should **not** be mixed into
anything we publish under ODbL, such as the artifact's snapshot. Keep the artifact snapshot adsb.lol-only, or ask
adsb.fi first (open question 2).

### 3.4 OpenSky Network REST

**API documentation** (<https://openskynetwork.github.io/opensky-api/rest.html>):
- "Anonymous users (unauthenticated, bucketed by IP): Only the most recent state vectors are available - the time
  parameter is ignored. Time resolution is 10 seconds"
- "Authenticated users: … Time resolution is 5 seconds"
- Credits per endpoint bucket: Anonymous **400/day**, Standard user **4,000/day**, Active feeder **8,000/day**,
  Licensed user **14,400/hour**.
- `/states/all` with a bounding box of ≤ 25 sq° costs 1 credit.
- "OpenSky exclusively supports the OAuth2 client credentials flow."
- State vectors have no registration, type, `nav_qnh` or heading. `position_source` is 0 = ADS-B, 1 = ASTERIX,
  2 = MLAT, 3 = FLARM.

**Terms** (<https://opensky-network.org/about/terms-of-use>):
> "Operational REST API use: Use of the REST API in any operational capacity — including integration into a live
> product, service, or automated system (even if only internal) — requires a previous written agreement, even for
> non-profit or governmental entities."

> §1: licence "solely for the purpose of non-profit research and non-profit education."

> §3(vi): "The REST API is provided for non-profit research and educational use only. Use of the REST API in any
> operational capacity — including but not limited to integration into a live product, service, or automated system —
> requires a written license from OpenSky Network, regardless of the entity's non-profit status."

> §3(iii): "You will not distribute, disclose, transfer or otherwise make available the data set(s) to any person other than those employed by your institute …"

**Observed:** four anonymous bounding-box probes (37.2–38.0 N, 122.9–121.8 W) — see section 4.8.
- `time` was **2.3–17.7 s** behind the response.
- `time` was never a multiple of 10 (…146, …179, …809, …826), although the documentation describes 10 s rounding.
- 15–16 states against 30–36 in adsb.fi's view of the same box.
- **5–6 ground aircraft within 3 km, against 18–22** in adsb.fi.
- Some `time_position` values were up to 397 s old. That is despite the documentation's "null if no position report
  was received by OpenSky within the past 15s".
- `X-Rate-Limit-Remaining` read 325, 363, 375, 360. It rose between probes, so other clients probably share this
  egress IP's anonymous bucket (inferred).

**Verdict:** the licence rules out the live app. It is acceptable only for one-off research comparisons like this one.

### 3.6 FlightAware AeroAPI and Firehose

**Documented** (<https://www.flightaware.com/commercial/aeroapi/>):

Tiers:
- Personal: "Storage and distribution of derivative works for personal or academic purposes only"; "Per-query usage
  fees (up to $5 free per month, or $10 free per month for ADS-B feeders)"; no minimum; "10 result sets/minute".
- Standard: $100/month minimum, "5 result sets/second".
- Premium: $1,000/month minimum, "100 result sets/second".

Fees:
- "GET /flights/search/positions Search for flight positions $0.050/result set"
- "GET /flights/{id}/position Get flight's current position $0.010/result set"
- "GET /airports/{id}/flights/arrivals … $0.005/result set"

A result set is up to 15 records ("15 responses = 1 page"). That comes from FlightAware staff on the forum,
<https://discussions.flightaware.com/t/what-is-considered-a-result-set-using-flights/85117>, so it is secondary.

The AeroAPI OpenAPI (v4.17.1) flight objects include `gate_destination` ("Arrival gate at the destination airport"),
`terminal_*` and `actual_runway_on`/`actual_runway_off`. Its `updateType` values include
"X=surface and near surface (ADS-B and ASDE-X)".

Firehose (<https://www.flightaware.com/commercial/firehose/>): "an enterprise grade streaming API that fuses airborne
and surface ADS-B positi[ons]". "Total monthly pricing is established on a per customer basis"; a free trial exists.

**Inferred:** tracking ~100 aircraft at 1 Hz through AeroAPI is impossible on the Personal tier (10 result sets/min),
and costs ~$0.35 per poll on paid tiers.

**Useful role:** per-flight **arrival gate and runway ground truth** for validating stand matching and runway
detection. At $0.005 per 15 arrivals, the free $5/month covers ~15,000 arrival records. Derivative works must stay
personal or academic.

### 3.8 AeroDataBox

- It is a flight status / FIDS / schedule API. Flight objects can carry
  "`location`: Real-time flight location, altitude, speed and track, if requested and available". It is not a
  surveillance feed.
- Free Basic plan: 400 units/month at 1 request/s, with attribution "Required".
- Caching: "Standard (7 days)".
- "Can I resell your API or any data from it? No — on any plan, free or paid. You cannot give, sell, or pass any third
  party access to the AeroDataBox API or to the data it returns."

**Verdict:** possible gate/terminal enrichment. It is not a position source.

## 4. Measurements

### 4.1 Method

**Recorder** (`tools/live/record.py`, PID in `refs/cache/rec/recorder.pid`):
- Polls both aggregators at 1 Hz within 40 NM of the ARP.
- adsb.fi uses the deprecated v2 `lat/lon/dist` endpoint; adsb.lol uses `/v2/point`.
- Each line stores `t` = the local time the request **started**, plus the full response.
- It backs off after errors (+2, 6, 14, 30, 60 s).

**Clock.** Request start minus the adsb.lol `now` ranged from −0.88 s to about +0.2 s (p1 −0.48 s, p99 +0.18 s)
[corrected by verifier: one outlier reached +3.30 s. It is the only value above +0.5 s among 1,970 OK responses; p99 is +0.17 s]. Given a
0.5 s server cache and ~0.3–0.5 s round trips, the local clock and adsb.lol's agree to within about half a second
(inferred). A finer clock check was not possible here.

**Analysis** (`tools/live/analyze_feeds.py`):
- It merges both streams in time order.
- Definitions:
  - *approach* = airborne, `alt_baro` < 5000 ft, within 10 NM of the ARP (37.6188056, −122.3754167);
  - *ground* = `alt_baro == "ground"` within 3 km of the ARP;
  - *new position* = `now − seen_pos` advanced by more than 0.05 s.
- Update intervals are therefore **lower-bounded by the polling rate** (1 s) and by each server's cache step:
  2 s for adsb.fi, 0.5 s for adsb.lol.
- Runway assignment uses the FAA/AirNav runway ends in `js/geo.js`. Each arrival's airborne points from the last
  150 s are scored by median |cross-track| against every runway direction.
- The geoid comparison uses EGM96 (PROJ grid `us_nga_egm96_15.tif`): N = −32.29 m at the ARP.
- Time percentiles are histogram-bin centres: 0.1 s bins for ages and 0.25 s bins for intervals. Hence values such as
  0.25 s or 1.125 s.

**Recording caveat.** The recorder was restarted at 07:28 UTC. Its hourly files therefore contain a truncated gzip
member followed by a new one. `analyze_feeds.py` has its own reader that recovers both members (open question 6).
[corrected by verifier] The original claim was that `tools/live/recio.py` "stops at the first corrupt block and yields
nothing for such files". That is wrong for the `recio.py` in the tree: file mtime 07:33:58 UTC, committed in dbdaf2b at
07:34:30 UTC, with no local diff. That version resynchronises at the next gzip header. Re-run on the 07 UTC files, it

### 4.2 Server cadence and latency

**adsb.fi** serves a new snapshot every **2.0 s**, on even seconds. So at 1 Hz, every second response is a duplicate.
Our request started a median ~0.8 s after its `now`.

**adsb.lol** snapshots are cached in **0.5 s** steps and are generated during our request (request start − `now` ≈ 0).
So the best-case age of an approach position at request time is:
- adsb.lol: p50 ≈ 0.15 s;
- adsb.fi: p50 ≈ 0.65 s.

**Wall-clock delay at the relay.** Add the network round trip (0.26–0.46 s measured) to the figures above. Using the
approach position age at request start (p50–p90), the total is **~0.5–1 s for adsb.lol and ~1–1.5 s for adsb.fi**.

**Not measurable here:** the feeder→aggregator delay, because `seen_pos` is counted from the aggregator's own receipt
time.

### 4.3 Update rate per aircraft

The interval between new positions is limited by our 1 Hz polling and by the server step.
- adsb.fi: 2.1 s (its 2 s step).
- adsb.lol: 1.1 s p50, with a long tail (p90 4.1 s) caused by the 429 back-offs.
- **Merged: 1.1 s p50 and 2.1 s p90**, both for approaches and for taxiing.

Stationary ground aircraft: p90 ≈ 5.1 s on adsb.fi. That matches the 0.2 Hz surface rate when still. The p99 values
are 10–26 s.

### 4.4 Cross-provider agreement

**Same report.** When both providers carry the same report (same position time, 46 % of pairs), the positions are
identical: p50 0 m, p99 0.6 m. `alt_baro` was identical too. The aggregators share feeders.

**Different reports.** After dead-reckoning the older one:
- airborne: p50 5 m, p99 29 m;
- taxiing: p50 2 m, p99 20 m;
- stationary: p50 0 m, p99 5 m.

These gaps are well within what the renderer must smooth anyway.

**Freshness.** adsb.lol was fresher in 48 % of pairs, the two were equal in 46 %, and adsb.fi was fresher in 6 %.

**Coverage.** adsb.fi carried **~1–3 ground aircraft that adsb.lol did not** (2,175 aircraft-snapshots against 25 the
other way). These were parked aircraft at the gates, with positions about 5 s old (p50) and 35 s (p90). This is the
main reason to merge rather than fail over.

### 4.6 Altitude and QNH

**`nav_qnh`.** Below 10,000 ft it was 1011.2 hPa, against the METAR altimeter of A2986 = 1011.3 hPa. The difference was
−0.1 hPa for every report, which is the field's 0.8 hPa quantisation. [corrected by verifier] A2986 is
29.86 inHg × 33.8639 = **1011.18 hPa**, not 1011.3. The 1011.3 is the `altim` field of the aviationweather.gov data
API, which returns 1011.3 for every A2986 METAR (re-fetched 09:1x UTC). So `nav_qnh` (1011.2) matches the reported
altimeter setting to +0.02 hPa. The −0.1 hPa is an artefact of the API's `altim` value, not of quantisation.
`nav_qnh` does step in 0.8 hPa (readsb `mode_s.c`: `800.0 + (baro_bits - 1) * 0.8`), and 1011.2 is the nearest step.
Using 1011.18 instead of 1011.3 moves the `alt_geom` − QNH-altitude medians below by +3.4 ft (−71.7 → −68.3 ft below
1000 ft). The conclusions are unchanged. Above FL200 it was 1012.8/1013.6 (standard
setting). So for the barometric fallback, the **METAR altimeter** is a sound single QNH below 18,000 ft:
`H = Hp + 145366.45·(1 − (1013.25/QNH)^0.190284)` ft. The aircraft's `nav_qnh` is a cross-check.
[verifier note] The altimeter-setting definition (ISA height of the pressure level minus ISA height of the setting)
gives a constant offset: `H = Hp − 145366.45·(1 − (QNH/1013.25)^0.190284)` ft. The form above differs from it by a
constant that does not depend on height: 0.02 ft at 1011.3 hPa, 5.9 ft at 980 hPa and 3.6 ft at 1040 hPa. That is
negligible here, but the relay should use the exact form.

**`alt_geom`.** We compared `alt_geom` with the QNH-corrected `alt_baro`, split by height band.
- **Below 1000 ft** the difference is p50 −72 ft (p10 −97, p90 −47; n = 1,727). All 22 aircraft had medians between
  −47 and −122 ft. An ellipsoidal (HAE) `alt_geom` predicts **−106 ft** (the EGM96 geoid undulation at the ARP is
  −32.29 m); an MSL one predicts 0.
- **Conclusion:** `alt_geom` is HAE, as readsb documents. The remaining ~+30 ft comes from 25 ft quantisation in both
  fields and altimeter/static error.
- Touchdown cross-check (observed): the A321 in section 4.5 read `alt_geom` −100 ft on the runway. That is +6 ft MSL
  after the geoid correction, against a runway elevation of ~13 ft. The four arrivals' last airborne `alt_geom`,
  converted to MSL, was 6–31 ft.
- **Higher up**, the difference grows steadily with height: −47 ft at 1000–1500 ft, +3 ft at 2000–3000 ft and
  +128 ft at 4000–5000 ft. This is the barometric altimeter's temperature error in the warm air above the night
  inversion.
- **Inferred:** a QNH-corrected `alt_baro` would draw aircraft on a 3° path over 100 ft too low at 4000+ ft. The
  geometric height would not.
- Method note: pooling all heights below 5000 ft mixes in this temperature error and wrongly suggests an MSL reference.
  A first pass of this analysis made exactly that mistake; judge the reference only below 1000 ft.

**Recommendation:**
- Vertical position = `alt_geom` + 105.9 ft → MSL.
- When `alt_geom` is missing, use the QNH-corrected `alt_baro` plus the aircraft's last `alt_geom − alt_baro` offset.
- Smooth with `baro_rate` (95–96 % present). `geom_rate` was present in only 5–7 % of approach reports.
- Pin the aircraft to the runway surface once it is on the ground.
- Sample: 22 aircraft overnight (every per-aircraft median between −47 and −122 ft); re-run on a daytime window.

### 4.7 Runway, take-off and go-around detection

**Runway assignment was unambiguous for every event.**
- Arrivals: 4 (28R 2, 28L 2). The median |cross-track| to the chosen runway was 2–5 m; the runner-up (the parallel
  runway) was 226–233 m.
- That matches the 228.6 m (750 ft) spacing computed from the FAA/AirNav runway ends in `js/geo.js`, for both 28L/28R
  and 1L/1R.

**Departures.** 14 (28L 13, 28R 1). Runway fit p50 6.7 m. The gap from the last ground report to the first airborne
report was 1.6 s p50, with one outlier of 15 s.

**Overnight flow.** It was 28R/28L for both arrivals and departures, consistent with the METAR wind 290°/5 kt.

**Go-arounds.** The detector looks for an aircraft aligned with a runway within 250 m cross-track and below 700 ft,
then climbing 1000 ft without a ground report. It found none yet.

**Inferred:** the feed supports runway, take-off and go-around classification at 1 Hz with a large margin.

## 5. Implementation guidance (relay and client)

### 5.1 Relay polling (`sfo_live_server.py`)

- **adsb.lol:**
  - Request `/v2/point/37.6188/-122.3754/40` every 1.0–1.5 s, one request in flight at a time.
  - On a 429, skip ≥ 2 s, then back off exponentially up to 30 s.
  - Send a descriptive User-Agent with a contact.
  - Contact the maintainer before any "production" use, as the ToS asks.
  - 40 NM covers downwind and base for all SFO approaches; 30 NM halves the payload. At night a response was
    ~13–15 kB of compact JSON; daytime will be several times larger.
- **adsb.fi:**
  - Request `/api/v3/lat/37.6188/lon/-122.3754/dist/40` every 2.0 s, timed ~0.3 s after an even second.
  - Never faster than 1 request/s. A 429 counts toward their temporary IP restriction.
- **Local receiver (optional):** `http://<pi>:<api-port>/?circle=37.6188,-122.3754,40&jv2` every 0.5–1 s.
- **Routes:** batch `POST /api/0/routeset` only for new callsigns, and cache them per callsign per day.
- **Enrichment (optional):** AeroAPI `/airports/KSFO/flights/arrivals` every few minutes, for `gate_destination` and
  `actual_runway_on`. This is Personal tier: keep it out of anything published.

### 5.2 Merge (per ICAO hex, lower-cased; keep `~` non-ICAO ids separate per provider)

```text
for each provider snapshot S (now_S in s) and aircraft a with lat/lon:
    pos_t = now_S − a.seen_pos
    if pos_t > merged[a.hex].pos_t + 0.05 s:            # strictly fresher position
        merged.pos ← (lat, lon, alt_baro, gs, track, true_heading, baro_rate, geom_rate, alt_geom, nav_qnh, type)
        merged.pos_t ← pos_t ; merged.src ← provider
    elif |pos_t − merged.pos_t| ≤ 0.05 s:               # same report from both: keep either
        prefer source-type rank adsb_icao > adsb_icao_nt > adsr_icao > tisb_icao > adsc > mlat > other (readsb order)
    identity fields (flight, squawk, category, r, t, desc, ownOp, year, dbFlags):
        take from the freshest snapshot that has them; prefer adsb.fi for desc/ownOp/year (adsb.lol omits them)
    drop the live position when now − pos_t > 60 s (readsb moves it to lastPosition);
    on ground near a stand keep a "parked" ghost (existing traffic.js persistence), since 10 of 22 vanishings were
    stationary aircraft at the gates.
```

### 5.3 Client

- Treat positions as **timestamped samples**: `t = pos_t`, not the arrival time.
- Either render ~1–2 s behind real time and interpolate, or dead-reckon from the newest sample with `gs`/`track`/
  `baro_rate` and blend corrections over ~1 s.
- With merged intervals of p50 1.1 s and p90 2.1 s, a 1.5 s playout delay would place ~90 % of samples before they are
  needed (inferred).
- Near the ground, use the approach geometry and the geometric height (`alt_geom` + 105.9 ft). Do not wait for the
  late `"ground"` flag.
- Heading on the ground, in order of preference: `true_heading` (2.8° steps), then `track` when moving, then the last
  known heading, then the existing inference.
- **Privacy (optional):** readsb `dbFlags & 8` marks LADD aircraft and `& 4` marks PIA. FAA SWIM data *must* honour
  LADD. The community feeds do not require it; the owner could choose to hide identity for those flags.
